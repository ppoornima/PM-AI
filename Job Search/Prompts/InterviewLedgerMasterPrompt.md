# Interview Ledger — Master Prompt

Attach the interview transcript, the role description when available, and `Interview-Ledger.xlsx`. Replace bracketed metadata when known.

---

You are operating my longitudinal Interview Ledger and course-correction system.

Analyze the attached transcript for `[COMPANY] — [ROLE] — [DATE] — [ROUND]`, produce a concise teardown, and update `Interview-Ledger.xlsx`. Use the role description and prior ledger entries when available. If the workbook does not exist, create it with these sheets in order: Dashboard, Interviews, Question Bank, Question Diagnostics, Patterns, Competitive Assets, Lists.

If metadata is unknown, do not block. Use blank, Unknown, Not Tested, Not Relevant, or Insufficient Evidence. Never invent an observation because a field exists.

## Goal

I am positioning for Lead / Principal product scope, particularly Applied AI or AI-adjacent roles, AI-native companies, platform roles, and ambiguous high-learning-curve environments.

Evaluate:

> What evidence did this conversation give them that I can operate one level above my current role, and what evidence weakened that belief?

For AI roles, also ask:

> Did I show that I can enter a partially unfamiliar technical/product domain, reason from first principles, learn quickly, ask the right questions, and translate uncertainty into a tractable product decision?

Treat the interview as a simulation of working with me. Infer from observable behavior whether I create clarity, take a position, listen, update, challenge intelligently, decide, bring people with me, and increase confidence in the room.

This is a course-correction tool, not a report. Keep the primary teardown to a five-minute read.

## Evidence rules

- Distinguish transcript fact, direct inference, analytical extension, and speculation.
- Cite timestamps when available.
- Do not manufacture depth, weakness, or praise.
- Reward demonstrated judgment, not senior vocabulary.
- Do not confuse lack of knowledge with poor reasoning, confidence with seniority, verbosity with depth, or strategy language with strategic judgment.
- A pass does not prove strong interviewing; a rejection does not prove weak interviewing.
- Outcome is later evidence, not permission to rewrite the transcript assessment.

## 1. Calibrate the interview

Identify separately:

- Role archetype: Core PM / Growth PM / Platform PM / Consumer-Fintech PM / Applied AI PM / AI-Native PM / Founding PM / Pod-of-One / Other.
- Primary interview format: Recruiter Screen / Hiring Manager / Product Sense / Execution / Strategy / Behavioral-Leadership / Analytics / Technical-Systems / AI Product / Ambiguous Case / Presentation / Executive-Final / Founder Conversation / Other.
- Secondary format, only if material.
- Interviewer lens: Product / Engineering / Design / Data / GTM-Sales / Founder / Executive / Recruiter / Cross-Functional / Other.
- What the conversation actually tested.

Do not use one generic PM rubric. Weight a weakness by the competency that the format was designed to test. For Founding PM or Pod-of-One roles, emphasize creating structure, autonomy, resource-constrained prioritization, customer discovery, product/technical/GTM breadth, speed of learning, imperfect decisions, and movement between strategy and execution.

Answer separately:

1. General capability gap or format-specific weakness?
2. Poor performance in a fitting role, or evidence of weaker role-archetype fit?

## 2. Interview thesis

Return this first:

| Question | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Level I sounded like | Sr- / Sr / Sr+ / Lead+ |
| Overall performance | Strong / Mixed / Weak / Insufficient Evidence |
| Role-fit signal | Strong / Mixed / Weak / Insufficient Evidence |
| Strongest reason to hire me | One sentence |
| Biggest interviewer doubt | One sentence |
| What made me distinctive | One sentence or blank |
| What capped my seniority | One mechanism |
| Highest-ROI change | One observable behavior |

Then state:

`My edge is ___; my current ceiling is ___; the behavior I am testing next is ___.`

## 3. Did I steer the ship?

Classify: Reactive / Participating / Co-shaping / Leading / Not Tested.

Look for useful reframing, better decision frames, surfaced assumptions, system connections, questions that changed the discussion, constructive challenge, synthesis, memorable insight, and anticipation. Cite at most three moments.

Identify one missed steering opportunity where I could have elevated the conversation without hijacking it.

## 4. Seniority and judgment

Assess judgment, prioritization, tradeoffs, strategic framing, organizational influence, ambiguity handling, ownership, systems thinking, simplification, and ability to create direction.

Ask: Did I explain what happened, or demonstrate how I think and decide?

Identify only the one or two behaviors most limiting Lead/Principal perception.

## 5. Executive communication and working-with-me signal

Evaluate:

- Clarity / Compression: did I make thinking easier to follow?
- Authority / Judgment: did I take a clear, defensible, updateable position?
- Confidence under challenge: did I stay grounded without bluffing or over-qualifying?
- Influence / Steering: did I improve the direction or quality of discussion?
- Leadership Presence: did I create clarity, direction, productive tension, and confidence?

Return only: strongest signal; biggest limiter; evidence; likely mechanism; one correction; and one sentence describing what it would likely feel like to solve a difficult problem with me.

Do not create separate spreadsheet scores for confidence, opinionatedness, fillers, hedging, verbosity, chronology, or answer length. These are diagnostic evidence beneath the stable dimensions.

## 6. AI and learning velocity

Only when relevant, evaluate:

- credible AI product/technical fluency without pretending to be an ML researcher
- connection of AI capabilities to user problems, workflow redesign, evals, failure modes, data, iteration loops, economics, trust/risk, and adoption
- ability to enter a new domain, build a mental model, experiment, and update
- separation of what I know, infer, and need to learn
- AI-native reasoning about probabilistic interfaces, changed marginal cost of cognition, iterative evaluation, agentic workflows, and less deterministic behavior

Return only meaningful findings; otherwise mark Not Relevant.

## 7. Unknown and unexpected questions

For important questions requiring live reasoning, evaluate whether I clarified, bounded the problem, stated assumptions, decomposed it, formed/tested a hypothesis, named evidence that would change the answer, and landed on a decision or next step.

Note bluffing, freezing, rambling, or premature solving only when supported. Judge whether the response increased or reduced confidence in my ability to operate without prior knowledge. Do not punish missing knowledge when reasoning was strong.

## 8. Strength to shadow

Identify at most one consequential example:

- Strength
- How it helps
- Shadow it creates or masks
- Where it limits the next level
- What to preserve
- What to change

Inspect whether technical depth masks abstraction, fluency masks structure, collaboration masks indecision, adaptability masks lack of POV, execution crowds out strategy, warmth becomes approval-seeking, comprehensiveness blocks prioritization, or quick thinking replaces depth. Do not manufacture a shadow.

## 9. Question capture and diagnostics

Put every substantive interviewer question in Question Bank using actual wording or a faithful concise paraphrase. Exclude small talk and administration. Preserve timestamps. Keep each original even when similar questions share a normalized Question Family and Recurrence Key.

For only diagnostically useful questions, return:

| Question / timestamp | Type and what tested | Result | Behavior tags | Root-cause hypothesis | Better answer shape |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Behavior tags can be multi-select: Hedging; Fillers-Interfering; Delayed Thesis; Excess Context; Rambling; Weak Structure; Weak Tradeoff; Softened Ownership; Bluffing; Froze; Missed Clarification; Premature Solution; Implementation-First; Generic Answer; Weak Metric Logic; Defensive; Failed to Land; Strong Reframe; Strong Clarification; Strong Decomposition; Strong Synthesis; Clear Position; Updated Well; Strong Steering.

Only tag fillers when they materially interfere. Several tags may be symptoms of one mechanism; do not automatically create several weaknesses.

## 10. Patterns and root cause

Compare with prior interviews. Prefer mechanisms over symptoms. Classify mechanisms as Knowledge / Skill / Retrieval / Habit / Thinking / Status-Emotional / Preparation-System / Strength-Shadow.

Thresholds:

- One occurrence: Observation
- Two supported occurrences: Emerging
- Three or more across interviews or contexts: candidate for Active or Structural

Use judgment for unusually strong evidence, but explain an early promotion. Return at most three recurring patterns with context trigger, affected formats, confidence, and trend.

## 11. The 20 percent

Give only two or three changes:

| Change | Why high leverage | What I do differently | Evidence it worked |
|---|---|---|---|

Then state what not to spend time fixing yet.

## 12. Next interview

Maximum five items:

- KEEP: one working behavior
- STOP: one observable behavior
- START: one replacement behavior
- REP: at most two question types, using structures rather than scripts
- EXPERIMENT: one testable behavior with a measurable success criterion

Keep one active experiment when possible so we can interpret causality.

## 13. Contrarian read

Challenge the one or two strongest conclusions. Offer another plausible interpretation. Distinguish communication problem, capability problem, experience gap, and role-fit issue. State confidence Low / Medium / High.

## Workbook update rules

### Interviews

Create one row per interview conversation, capturing metadata, format/archetype/lens, performance, role fit, level signal, hire edge, interviewer doubt, memorable contribution, working-with-me signal, seven stable dimensions, one primary constraint, one high-leverage fix, one next experiment and success criterion, references, confidence, and later outcome.

### Question Bank

Store every substantive interviewer question with company, role archetype, format, lens, exact/paraphrased question, timestamp, normalized family, what it tests, performance, preparedness, answer gap, best answer shape, prep priority, and recurrence key.

### Question Diagnostics

Store only questions that reveal a strong signal, consequential failure, unknown-handling behavior, steering opportunity, or recurring mechanism. Behavior Tags is a semicolon-separated multi-select field.

### Patterns

Store mechanisms, their type, context trigger, first/last seen, count, supporting interviews, affected formats, impact, root-cause hypothesis, intervention, result, trend, status, and confidence. Do not split long answers, excess context, delayed thesis, and interviewer reframing if one mechanism explains them.

### Competitive Assets

Store only repeated strengths that create meaningful advantage, with evidence, differentiation, relevant roles, shadow risk, deliberate deployment, occurrence count, status, and confidence. Do not store generic traits such as hardworking or smart.

### Dashboard

Maintain:

- Current Interview Thesis
- Performance by Interview Format
- Performance and Fit by Role Archetype
- Last-five tested values for Level Signal, Clarity / Compression, Authority / Judgment, Influence / Steering, Leadership Presence, Unknown Handling, and AI / Learning Signal
- Top Question Families to Prepare
- Active Mechanisms
- Current Experiment

Use anchored labels, not 1–10 scores. Do not average ordinal labels. Show distributions, latest signals, and supported trend direction. Reassess the Current Interview Thesis every four to five interviews.

## Data integrity

- Use stable IDs and upsert; do not duplicate an interview when the outcome arrives.
- Preserve the original transcript-based assessment when adding outcome or feedback.
- Increment a pattern or asset only for independent supporting evidence.
- Keep transcript and teardown references when available.
- Store only information that helps compare interviews, expose format-specific strengths/failures, diagnose mechanisms, judge level/fit, choose preparation, or test an intervention.

Before writing a field, ask:

1. Will this change a future decision?
2. Is it a symptom or mechanism?
3. Is it duplicated elsewhere?
4. Is it supported strongly enough for comparison?
5. Would I want to read it after 20 interviews?

If not, omit it.

## Later outcome updates

When result or interviewer feedback arrives later, update the same interview row with outcome, date, and explicit feedback. Recalculate format/archetype views and pattern evidence. Do not rewrite the original assessment to fit the result. If interpretation changes, record the revised view separately with date and rationale.

## Final response

Return the teardown first. Then state which workbook sheets/rows changed, which patterns/assets changed or stayed unchanged, the single active experiment, and any missing metadata or pending outcome. Do not dump the spreadsheet payload into chat unless the workbook could not be updated.
